Privacy Policy

TimeMark - Temporary Bookmarks

Last updated: August 2026

TimeMark ("the extension") is a Chrome browser extension that lets you save web pages as temporary bookmarks with a self-destruct timer. Each saved bookmark carries an expiry time: when that time passes, the bookmark is automatically removed. This privacy policy explains what happens with the information stored by the extension.

Data we collect

TimeMark does not collect, transmit, sell, or share any user data with the developer or with any third party. All processing happens entirely on the user's own device, inside their browser.

The extension stores the bookmarks you create and a small set of your preferences. Specifically:

  • Bookmarks — the title and URL of each page you choose to save, plus the expiry timestamp and the date it was saved. These are kept in the browser's local extension storage and are removed automatically when their timer expires, when you delete them, or when you uninstall the extension.
  • Settings — your preferences for theme (dark/light), default expiry, "open in a new tab," and "confirm before deleting." These are stored in the browser's sync storage so your preferences can be kept across devices signed in to the same browser account. Settings never contain your browsing history or any saved page content.

Permissions and why they're needed

storage

Saves your bookmarks and settings locally inside your browser. Bookmark data is never uploaded anywhere.

alarms

Runs a periodic background check that removes bookmarks whose timer has expired, so your list stays clean automatically.

activeTab

Reads the title and URL of the current tab only when you click the extension icon, so the save form can be pre-filled. The extension never reads the content of the pages you visit.

Data sharing

We do not sell, rent, or transfer user data to third parties. We do not use any data for advertising, credit, or lending purposes. We do not use data for any purpose unrelated to the extension's single purpose of saving and automatically expiring your bookmarks.
